📖 Complete Operating Guide

How to Use PlateVision

Learn how to set up your camera, run live plate detection, and get the best recognition results.

Quick Start

1

Connect Your Camera

Connect a supported webcam or camera and make sure it is visible to your computer before opening the dashboard.

2

Start the GPU Scanner

On the Dashboard, click Start camera. The system loads YOLO11x and EasyOCR directly into NVIDIA GPU VRAM and begins live inference.

3

Review Captured Plates

Recognized plates appear with bounding boxes and green text overlays. Logs automatically save to plate_records.json and can be exported as CSV or JSON.

Best Practices

💡

Lighting

Use even, diffuse lighting so the registration plate remains clear and readable.

📷

Camera Angle

Position the camera facing approaching vehicles at a shallow angle and avoid heavy glare.

🚗

Plate Condition

Standard registration plates provide the highest accuracy. Dusty or bent plates may reduce confidence scores.

🔎

Clear View

Keep the complete plate inside the camera frame and minimize motion blur.

Frequently Asked Questions

The video preview displays “The model stream could not start”?
Check that the camera is connected and not in use by another application, then restart the scanner.
Why is the FPS lower than expected?
Ensure your laptop is plugged into AC power so the NVIDIA GPU runs in full performance mode. Verify that PyTorch is using CUDA by running:
python -c "import torch; print(torch.cuda.is_available())"
If it returns False, install the CUDA 12.6 build of PyTorch.
Where are my saved records?
All records are saved permanently in plate_records.json in the project directory. You can also export them directly from the Dashboard as CSV or formatted JSON.
